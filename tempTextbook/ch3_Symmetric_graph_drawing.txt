### Geometric Automorphism Drawing Problem (GADP)
**Instance:** Graph \(G\), automorphism group \(A\) of \(G\) given by at most 2 generators.
**Output:** Straight-line drawing of \(G\) that displays \(A\) (if possible).

There is a linear-time algorithm that solves GADP (Corollary 3.2). It is given constructively by the proof of Theorem 3.2 (drawing on a circular grid). The three cases are:

**(a) Reflection (\(|A|=2\))**
- Draw \(V\setminus\operatorname{fix}_A\) on a circle about the origin so that the \(x\)-coordinates of \(u\) and \(\alpha(u)\) coincide; draw the induced edges as straight lines (axial symmetry about the \(y\)-axis, non-degenerate).
- The edges induced by \(V\setminus\operatorname{fix}_A\) cross the \(y\)-axis at finitely many places. Draw \(\operatorname{fix}_A\) on the \(y\)-axis (one path at a time) so that its vertices avoid those crossings.
- Draw the edges between \(V\setminus\operatorname{fix}_A\) and \(\operatorname{fix}_A\).

**(b) Rotation (\(A=\langle\rho\rangle\))**
- If \(\operatorname{fix}_A=\emptyset\), place every vertex on the unit circle. The orbits all have the same size \(k\). Choose a representative \(u_i\) from each orbit \(O_i\) and place \(\rho^j(u_i)\) at angle \(2\pi(i+jn/k)/n\).
- If \(\operatorname{fix}_A=\{c\}\), place \(c\) at the origin (the conditions of the theorem guarantee non-degeneracy).

**(c) Dihedral group (\(A=\langle\alpha,\rho\rangle\))**
- Use an \(n\times(n/k)\) circular grid.
- Draw each conjugate of \(\operatorname{fix}_\alpha\) on the appropriate pair of rays by rotating the drawing of \(\operatorname{fix}_\alpha\).
- Place the remaining orbits on the innermost circle, choosing representatives so that \(\alpha\) is displayed, then rotate by \(2\pi/k\).
- Non-degeneracy follows from Lemma 3.1 and the hypotheses of the theorem.

### Algorithm Max_PAG_tricon
(for a maximum-size planar automorphism group of a triconnected planar graph)

1. Find a face \(f\) of \(G\) such that the stabilizer \(\operatorname{stab}_A(f)\) is maximised.
2. Find the orbits of \(\operatorname{stab}_A(f)\).
3. Find generators of \(\operatorname{stab}_A(f)\).

**Implementation notes (linear time):**
- Step 1 uses Fontet’s algorithm on the dual to obtain a minimum-size face orbit; any face of that orbit may be chosen as the outer face.
- Star-triangulate internal faces, then (if necessary) add a surrounding hexagon so that the outer face has more than three vertices; re-apply Fontet’s algorithm.
- On the outer face vertices \(v_0,\dots,v_{m-1}\) locate three consecutive vertices \(v_i,v_j,v_k\) belonging to a non-trivial orbit with \(j-i\) minimal. Compute (via the flag lemma) the three candidate automorphisms \(\alpha,\rho_1,\rho_2\) and select the generators of the cyclic or dihedral group they generate.

**Lemma 3.3:** The algorithm runs in linear time.

### Algorithm MAX_PAG_bicon
(for a maximum-size planar automorphism group of a biconnected planar graph)

**Step 1.** Construct the SPQR-tree \(T\) of \(G\) (linear time via Hopcroft–Tarjan).

**Step 2. Reduction** (bottom-up, level by level from the leaves to the root)
(a) For each leaf node on level \(i\), compute labels on its parent virtual edge.
(b) Transfer those labels onto the corresponding virtual edge in the parent node.
(c) Delete the leaf nodes of level \(i\).

(The labels encode isomorphism codes, axial-swap flags, etc., so that every planar automorphism of the labelled reduced graph extends to the original graph.)

**Step 3.** Compute a maximum-size planar automorphism group at the (labelled) centre of \(T\).

(Seven families of labels and separate routines for each type of triconnected component / centre are required; details appear in the referenced papers.)

### One-connected and disconnected cases
- One-connected graphs are handled by an analogous reduction on the rooted BC-tree, calling the biconnected algorithm as a subroutine.
- Disconnected graphs are reduced to an arrangement-of-discs problem (isomorphism classes of components treated as coloured polygonal discs, possibly with holes). The maximum dihedral symmetry size is obtained from a constant number of gcd computations (Lemma 3.4 bounds the number of non-conjugate maximal groups by 3).

### Drawing algorithms (Section 3.5.5)
Given a planar graph and a planar automorphism group \(A\):
- **Triconnected:** linear-time straight-line drawing (more sophisticated than Tutte’s barycentre method) that realises \(A\); supports a “squash” operation at a prescribed outer-face vertex that preserves axial symmetry.
- **Biconnected:** star-triangulate to obtain a triconnected supergraph that still admits \(A\), then apply the triconnected drawer.
- **One-connected:** draw the root block, then attach successive blocks of the BC-tree, scaling/squashing them into faces of already-drawn blocks.

**Theorem 3.7:** A straight-line drawing that displays \(A\) can be produced in linear time. (Worst-case area is exponential.)
