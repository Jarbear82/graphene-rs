The document is a survey of results on simultaneous embeddings of planar graphs (SGE, SEFE, and SE). It contains constructive proofs and high-level algorithmic descriptions for several positive results, but **no formal pseudocode**. Below is pseudocode reconstructed directly from the explicit constructive arguments given in the text (primarily Theorems 11.1 and 11.6, together with the supporting constructions they invoke).

### 1. Simultaneous Geometric Embedding of Two Paths
(Theorem 11.1 / Brass et al. [BCD⁺07])

```
Algorithm TwoPathsSGE(P₁, P₂)
Input:  Two paths P₁ = (v_{π₁(1)}, …, v_{π₁(n)}) and
        P₂ = (v_{π₂(1)}, …, v_{π₂(n)}) on the same vertex set V of size n
Output: Straight-line drawings of P₁ and P₂ on an n × n grid
         with identical vertex positions

1. for each vertex u ∈ V do
2.     let p₁ ← position of u in P₁          // p₁ ∈ {1,…,n}
3.     let p₂ ← position of u in P₂          // p₂ ∈ {1,…,n}
4.     place u at the grid point (p₁, p₂)
5. draw every edge of P₁ as a straight-line segment
6. draw every edge of P₂ as a straight-line segment
7. return the two drawings
```

**Correctness note (from the text).**
P₁ becomes an *x*-monotone polygonal chain and P₂ becomes a *y*-monotone polygonal chain; neither can self-intersect.

### 2. Simultaneous Embedding (with bends) of Two Planar Graphs
(Theorem 11.6 / Erten & Kobourov [EK05a])

The algorithm first handles the 4-connected case, then reduces the general case to it via the Kaufmann–Wiese augmentation.

```
Algorithm SimultaneousEmbedding(G₁, G₂)
Input:  Two planar graphs G₁ = (V,E₁), G₂ = (V,E₂) on n vertices
Output: Drawings of G₁ and G₂ on an O(n²)×O(n²) grid
         with at most three bends per edge and identical vertex positions

// ---------- Phase 1: 4-connected case ----------
1. if G₁ and G₂ are both 4-connected then
2.     compute Hamiltonian cycles H₁ of G₁ and H₂ of G₂
        (Chiba–Nishizeki algorithm)
3.     let P₁ ⊂ H₁ and P₂ ⊂ H₂ be the corresponding Hamiltonian paths
4.     compute an SGE of P₁ and P₂ by TwoPathsSGE(P₁,P₂)
        // P₁ is y-monotone, P₂ is x-monotone
5.     δ ← minimum absolute slope among all edges of the drawing of P₁
6.     δ' ← a value slightly smaller than δ
7.     // close the Hamiltonian cycles and add the remaining edges
8.     for each missing edge e of H₁ (and later of G₁\H₁) do
9.         draw e by two straight-line segments of slopes ±δ' (or
            slopes chosen from the open intervals (δ',δ) and (−δ,−δ'))
10.    treat G₂ analogously (using the x-monotone path P₂)
11.    return the resulting drawings
        // each edge has at most one bend

// ---------- Phase 2: general planar graphs ----------
12. else
13.     augment G₁ (resp. G₂) to a 4-connected planar graph G₁⁺ (G₂⁺)
        by adding edges and subdividing each original edge at most once
        (Kaufmann–Wiese technique)
14.     obtain drawings of G₁⁺ and G₂⁺ by the 4-connected procedure above
15.     delete all dummy edges
16.     replace every subdivision vertex by a bend
17.     return the resulting drawings
        // each original edge now has at most three bends
```

**Grid-size remark (from the text).**
All vertex and bend coordinates lie on an O(n²)×O(n²) integer grid (or O(n³)×O(n³) if every bend is required to be a grid point).

### 3. Additional constructive results that admit straightforward pseudocode

The survey also gives explicit (but shorter) constructions for several restricted families.  Their high-level structure is identical:

* **Caterpillar + path / two caterpillars / stars / cycles** (Brass et al.)
  Place the “spine” vertices by a path-style coordinate assignment, then attach leaves in constant-width corridors so that no two edges of the same graph cross.

* **Tree + matching / outerpath + matching / wheel + cycle** (Cabello et al.)
  First embed the tree (or outerpath) by a standard straight-line algorithm, then route the matching edges with a constant number of slopes that avoid the already-drawn faces.

* **Level-planar graph + path** (Cappos et al.)
  The path induces a layering; a standard level-planar straight-line drawing of the first graph already realises an SGE.

Because the survey only sketches the geometric ideas and does not supply line-by-line procedures for these families, the two algorithms above are the only ones for which complete, self-contained pseudocode can be extracted directly from the given text.
